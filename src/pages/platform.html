Voice

How we sound, wherever we show up: a homepage, a 404 page, a support reply or an email signature.

Not “bippity boppity boo” magic, but “wow, I can’t believe how well that worked” magic.

Do

  • Celebrate imagination with creative, powerful and unexpected words.
  • Write like music: short parts, medium parts and longer parts, with rhythm that builds and wanes.
  • Use contractions like we’re, you’ve and let’s.
  • Treat every touchpoint, even an error page, as a chance for a little surprise and delight.

Avoid

  • Over-explaining minutiae. Leave room for wonder.
  • Technical terms without explanation.
  • Harsh or divisive language. We’re here to create, not provoke.
  • Overpromising and hyperbole. They’re real magic killers.

Friendly and approachable, with major horsepower under the hood. We are not a toy.

Do

  • Share pull quotes and stories of regular people doing amazing things.
  • Use metaphors, the most efficient and impactful literary device.
  • Be brief. Brevity is power.
  • Use constructive words when suggesting or correcting.

Avoid

  • Blowing sunshine or pandering.
  • Memes. They move too fast for a global audience.
  • Emojis in messaging. They hurt credibility and are easy to misread.
  • Clichés, unless you give them an unexpected twist.

Humans have what robots can’t replicate: real emotion, lived experience and true creativity. We use that to build authentic connections.

Do

  • Use friendly, conversational language, like one person talking to another.
  • Ask questions to draw the reader in.
  • Be colorful, with bright words that light up the reader’s mind.
  • Make it about them, not us. The user is the hero.

Avoid

  • Shipping AI-written copy. A first pass is fine; a human always proofs for human-ness.
  • Being overly formal or scripted.
  • Being a grammar stickler when the meaning is clear.
  • Stacking exclamation points.

Guiding isn’t leading or directing. It meets you where you are, helps when you need it and lets you cook when you don’t.

Do

  • Speak to the middle of the bell curve. Many readers don’t speak English as a first language.
  • Use we, us, let’s and together. No one builds alone.
  • Talk directly to the Builder.
  • Let real enthusiasm show.

Avoid

  • Condescending. We all start somewhere.
  • Calling things “easy,” “intuitive” or “simple” unless they objectively are.
  • Assuming people know more, or less, than they do.
  • Messaging more often than people want.

Tone

Our voice stays the same; our tone shifts with the moment. Brand and marketing can be more playful and enthusiastic. In-product and docs lean more matter-of-fact. Never negative, silly, hollow or sycophantic.

How to describe Netlify

Approved descriptions for press, partner pages, event programs and bios. Use the length that fits.

One line

Netlify is a platform that lets you create, share, and run anything on the web.

Short

Netlify is a platform for creating, collaborating on, and running web apps. It has tons of pre-packaged tools so that anyone—from those just starting out to seasoned developers—can create powerful tools for the web without any of the friction.

Long

Netlify is all about helping people build incredibly useful things for the web. It can be a website, an app, an agent experience—anything they can dream up. Using their own tools or using ours, people can build, collaborate with their team, get the thing up and running, and keep it that way. Netlify is where anyone, from aspiring builders to seasoned devs, creates pro-level web experiences that really work, really well.
